Key findings

  • F-12 H's downhole gauge failed on 12 Oct 2010 and logged zeros for almost 6 years. Left in, those zeros made the average pressure look 3 times too low (83.9 instead of 256.9).
  • The 20 'impossible' 25-hour days are real: every one is the last Sunday of October, when Norway turns its clocks back. They were kept, not deleted.
  • Water broke through in 2010: field water cut jumped from 8 % to 53 % in one year, and the yearly average stayed at 70 % or higher from 2011 on.
  • New wells in 2013-2014 (F-11 H, F-15 D, F-1 C) coincide with higher oil output and a lower water cut in 2014-2015.
  • An Arps exponential decline fitted F-14 H with R² 0.975. On 13 hidden months it over-predicted oil by 15 %: the well declined faster than the curve.
  • The daily-to-monthly roll-up matches Equinor's own monthly sheet (e.g. F-1 C, April 2014: 11,142.5 oil, 227.5 hours).

Method and limitations

  • QC rules: never invent data. Remove what is physically impossible, keep what is proven real, flag what cannot be decided.
  • Units are not stated in the daily sheet. Volumes are assumed to be Sm³ (as in Equinor's monthly sheet); pressure units are not stated.
  • Known limitation: zero-pressure values were removed only on producing days. F-12 H zeros on shut-in days after 11 Oct 2010 are also likely gauge failures and remain in the clean data.
  • Missing days (228) are listed in the QC log, not filled in.
